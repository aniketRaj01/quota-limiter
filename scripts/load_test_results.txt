=== Reading these numbers ===
At concurrency=1, a full HTTP round trip through the whole stack (idempotency check, atomic
deduct, simulated downstream, refund-if-needed, response caching) is well under 1ms — consistent
with the ~0.013ms in-process check_and_consume-only benchmark in tests/test_quota_latency.py plus
a small, expected amount of ASGI/Pydantic/network overhead.

The <10ms per-operation target holds at low concurrency but degrades as concurrency rises, and
throughput itself peaks around concurrency=5 (~1260 req/s) then *declines* as concurrency
increases further (633 req/s at c=10, ~400-460 req/s at c=20-50). That's the signature of
queueing/contention overhead growing faster than the work being done, not any single operation
getting slower — the per-operation cost proven separately (test_quota_latency.py) never changed.

Root cause, per this build's own design (DESIGN-NOTES.md): every quota mutation is serialized
through one process-wide threading.Lock guarding the single :memory: SQLite connection. That
lock is correct and necessary for this build's atomicity guarantee, but it means total system
throughput is capped by (lock hold time + Python thread-scheduling/GIL overhead per acquisition)
regardless of how many requests arrive concurrently — and CPython's lock contention overhead
grows superlinearly with the number of waiting threads, which is exactly what the sweep shows.
This is the honest limitation the multi-instance/Redis scale-out section of DESIGN-NOTES.md
exists to address: a single in-process lock is a real ceiling around a few hundred to ~1000
req/s on this hardware, well below what 50,000 orgs would require.
